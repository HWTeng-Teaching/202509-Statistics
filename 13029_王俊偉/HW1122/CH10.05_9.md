$S^2=\frac{\sum{X_i^2}-\frac{(\sum{X_i}^2}{n}}{n-1}=\frac{48.95-\frac{17.7^2}{7}}{7-1} \approx 0.70$  

(2)  
$\alpha=1-0.95=0.05$  
CI for $\sigma^2$  
$=[\frac{(n-1)S^2}{X^2_{(n-1),\alpha/2}}，\frac{(n-1)S^2}{X^2_{(n-1),1-\alpha/2}}]$  
$=[\frac{(7-1)0.7}{X^2_{(7-1),0.05/2}}，\frac{(7-1)0.7}{X^2_{(7-1),1-0.05/2}}]$  
$=[\frac{4.2}{14.4494}，\frac{4.2}{1.237347}]$  
$\approx [0.29，3.39]$  

(3)  
It's a two-side test.  
$H_0：\sigma^2=0.8$  
$H_1：\sigma^2 \neq 0.8$  
$\alpha=0.05$  
$X^2_{STAT}=\frac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2} \sim X^2_{(n-1)}$  
$X^{2*}=\frac{(7-1)0.7}{0.8}=5.25$  
RR={ $X^2：X^2 < 1.237347$ or $X^2 >14.4494$ }  
$X^{2*}=5.25$ doesn't fall in RR.  
$\therefore$ Don't reject $H_0$.  
There is not sufficient evidence to conclude that the population variance is different from 0.8.
