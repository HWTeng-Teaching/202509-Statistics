# a.<br>
n=50<br>
$\overline{x}=8.0$<br>
95%=100(1-a)% CI for $\mu$ => a=.05<br>
margin of error($n \geq 30$)=95% margin of error= $\pm 1.96 \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}=\pm 1.96 \frac{.3}{\sqrt{50}}=$<br>
$\pm .0832$<br>
=> $8.0 \pm .0832 => 7.9168 < \mu < 8.0832$ <br>

# b.<br>
n=50<br>
$\overline{x}=67.3$<br>
99%=100(1-a)% CI for $\mu$ => a=.01<br>
99% margin of error = $\pm 2.58 \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}=\pm 2.58 \frac{.64}{\sqrt{50}}=$<br>
$\pm .2335$<br>
=> $67.3 \pm .2335 => 67.0665 < \mu < 67.5335$ <br>

# c.<br>
1. The sampling distribution of the point estimator should be centered over the true value of the parameter to be estimated. Otherwise, the estimator is said to be biased.<br>
2. A second important characteristic is that the spread (as measured by the variance) of the estimator’s sampling distribution should be as small as possible.<br>
3. The **95% margin of error** when estimated **$n \geq 30$** is estimated as $\pm 1.96 \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}$<br>
4. The **99% margin of error** when estimated **$n > 30(n \geq 52?)$** is estimated as $\pm 2.58 \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}$<br>
